# Decision check: The Guest Register

This prompt tests SDD Milestone 17. Its question is whether a first-time
player chooses the next city for a reason they can state, and whether the
lookup they learned transfers to a name the case never gave them.

Run it as an addition to the [novice walkthrough](novice-walkthrough.md).
Paste the walkthrough prompt with [MISSION TITLES IN ORDER] set to _The Guest
Register_ and [EXPECTED CLEARANCE] set to 2. Then paste everything below the
line after it. Seed the save with `borrowed-chronometer` (see the
[README](README.md)).

---

## Two more things to do in The Guest Register

**Before you send the travel order from Luanda**, stop and write down, in
this person's words:

- which city you are about to send the order to;
- each fact on the screen that made you choose it, and where on the screen
  you saw it;
- for each other city, the fact that made you drop it, or that you dropped it
  without one.

Then send the order and carry on.

**In Zanzibar City, before you submit the deduction**, clear the editor
completely, so that nothing of the starter query is left. From the blank
editor, without looking at an earlier query, write a query that finds which
operative the registry files the cover name H. Brandt under. Transmit it,
and record whether it worked the first time, what you changed if it did
not, and the operative it found. Then finish the case.

## Add to your report

8. **The choice of city**: what you wrote before the travel order, word for
   word. Say whether you looked at the map or the case board while choosing,
   and whether either one changed what you did.
9. **The lookup from a blank editor**: the query as you first typed it,
   every change you made, and the result.

# Decision check: Three Couriers

This addition tests the short pursuit of SDD 3.4. Its question is whether a
player who has identified the courier can say why they followed him to the
city they chose, and whether the lookup they learned transfers to a name the
case never gave them.

Run it as an addition to the [novice walkthrough](novice-walkthrough.md).
Paste the walkthrough prompt with [MISSION TITLES IN ORDER] set to _Three
Couriers_ and [EXPECTED CLEARANCE] set to 3, and set [PRIOR PLAY] to: "You
have played the first four cases, and you remember what their debriefs
taught." Then paste everything below the line after it. Seed the save with
`quiet-consignee` (see the [README](README.md)).

---

## Two more things to do in Three Couriers

**Before you send the travel order that follows the courier**, stop and write
down, in this person's words:

- which city you are about to send the order to;
- each fact on the screen that made you choose it, and where on the screen
  you saw it;
- for each other city, the fact that made you drop it, or that you dropped it
  without one;
- whether you thought about ordering a field check on any sighting, what
  the screen said it would cost, and why you did or did not order one.

Then send the order and carry on.

**In Lusaka, before you submit the deduction**, clear the editor completely,
so that nothing of the starter query is left. From the blank editor, without
looking at an earlier query, write a query that finds which operative the
registry files the cover name P. Okello under. Transmit it, and record
whether it worked the first time, what you changed if it did not, and the
operative it found. Then finish the case.

## Add to your report

8. **The choice of city**: what you wrote before the travel order, word for
   word. Say whether you looked at the map or the case board while choosing,
   and whether either one changed what you did.
9. **The lookup from a blank editor**: the query as you first typed it,
   every change you made, and the result.
10. **The courier**: in this person's words, what happened to the courier
    after you reached Lusaka, and whether the case felt like a chase.
11. **The risk**: what you would have lost by going to the wrong city, and
    what a field check, if you ordered one, showed you. Say what made you
    want to follow the courier, or that nothing did.
