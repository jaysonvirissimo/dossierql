# Agent playtests of clearance 10, 23 November 2026

Four runs covered the two new cases, _The Inner Room_ and _The Envelope
Book_. Each case had one run of [clean-room-solve.md](../clean-room-solve.md)
and one of [novice-walkthrough.md](../novice-walkthrough.md). Each run used
its own isolated browser context against the preview build. The save was
seeded with _The Cipher Counter_, and for _The Envelope Book_ with _The Inner
Room_ as well. Every run started at clearance 10.

## How the runs differed from the prompts

- The runners were browser-only agents, but their system instructions named
  another site or another game. Each runner noticed this, kept to the page it
  was given, and played DossierQL. This happened in the clearance 6 to 9
  rounds as well.
- The seed promotes by placement, so the desk showed the earlier cases as
  unplayed. Nothing in either case depends on it.
- A page of 120 papers made the accessibility snapshot too large for two
  runners to read, so they sent the file again in smaller pages. Their counts
  below include those transmissions. Two runners could not give the query
  editor focus with a click from their tools, and typed into the variables
  panel by mistake before using the keyboard or the fill tool. This is
  logged below and was not reproduced by hand.

## Findings fixed

1. **The weights did not follow from the text** (all four runs). A request
   for one paper weighed 22 or 75, and the file of 40 instructions weighed
   121, the same as the file of 120 papers. The briefing said that "one
   minute sheet holds ten remarks", and every sheet a runner opened held one
   or two. The clerk counts a list for the longest it is anywhere in the
   archive (SDD 3.3), and no text said so for these files. Stage 1 now says
   that the clerk counts every minute sheet as ten, whatever it holds.
   Stage 2 says that a page with no page size counts as the longest file,
   120 papers, although this file holds 40. The descriptions of
   `PaperFile.papers` and `Paper.minuteSheet` say the same, and so does the
   debrief of _The Envelope Book_.
2. **Endorsements had no name in the draft** (all four runs). The draft
   listed a cited remark as "endorsement 7, not yet identified", and nothing
   on screen carried that number. `Endorsement` now has a `reference`, such
   as `END-0302`, so the findings and the draft both head it by that
   reference.
3. **Stage 1 did not say what values `first` and `after` take** (novice,
   _The Inner Room_). The briefing now says that `after` takes the reference
   of the paper just before the first one you want, and `first` the number
   of papers.
4. **"Its value" had two targets** (novice, _The Inner Room_, stage 2). The
   field's value and the variable's value were both possible readings. The
   briefing now says "the value of `if`".
5. **Stage 4 used a directive after a fragment spread without saying so**
   (novice, _The Inner Room_). The starter writes `...Sheet
@include(if: $detail)`, and only the documentation panel explained it. The
   briefing now says that a directive after a spread covers every field of
   the fragment, as the three dots with no type did in stage 3.
6. **The stage 2 claim was plural** (novice, _The Inner Room_). The objective
   asks about one instruction, and the claim said "the standing
   instructions". The claim is now singular.
7. **The debrief contradicted its own code** (novice, _The Inner Room_). "The
   reference solution never edits its operation" sat above operations that
   differ between stages. It now says that within each stage the reference
   solution changes only its variables.
8. **The assessment did not say what kind of record its book is, or the
   hour** (clean room, _The Envelope Book_). The briefing now says that the
   book is a file of papers, in which each envelope is a paper with a minute
   sheet. It also gives the hour of CRC-0096, 08:58, which tells the sealed
   envelope apart from a later envelope that is also to be called for.

## Findings logged

- **The assessment can be passed without a directive** (both runs of _The
  Envelope Book_). A light request and a narrow page with `after` solve it in
  two transmissions. This is the rule of SDD 3.7: the game accepts any query
  that yields enough evidence. Directives have no kata yet, so the debrief
  makes no technique note, as at clearances 6 to 9 (Section 3.10). The
  debrief shows the reference's `@skip(if: $summary)`. A kata for directives
  is the fix, and it is on the list after 1.0.
- **The enclosure's evidence is optional in the assessment** (clean room).
  The claim has two evidence sets, and the runner used the one on the
  envelope's own minute sheet. SDD 5.6 asks for two routes.
- **A long response is hard to read in the Findings tab** (three runs). The
  header already points to Side by side. This is the same finding as at
  clearance 9, and it waits for sessions with people.
- **A click on the query editor focused the variables panel** (two runs).
  This was not reproduced by hand, and the end-to-end tests focus the editor
  by its role. It may come from how the tools click a tall editor.
- **The stage 2 starter shows `$summary` underlined** (clean room). The
  editor's validation is right: the starter declares `$summary`, and nothing
  uses it until the player writes `@skip(if: $summary)`.
- **The objective follows the briefing's last sentence** (two runs). The two
  repeat each other on every case, by the layout of SDD 8.2.
- **Citing the remark alone left the claim needing evidence** (clean room,
  _The Inner Room_). The draft said that the claim needs more facts that
  connect to this one, and the runner cited the officer beside it. This is
  the citing mechanic of Section 3.2, unchanged since clearance 1.
- **The desk headed the first campaign after the last case** (novice, _The
  Envelope Book_). With the seed promoting by placement, the next case in
  the story is the first case of the game, and no case is filed above
  clearance 10.

## The novice walkthrough on the fixed build

The novice walkthrough of _The Inner Room_ was run again on the fixed build.
The runner solved it with no hints, in 9 transmissions against the
reference's 6, with 21 of 30 hours left. Three of the transmissions were
smaller pages that its tools needed in order to read 120 papers. It wrote
`@include` and `@skip` unaided, and chose between them by what the variable
means. It reported three more points, and all three were fixed:

1. Two papers in full weighed 43, and the briefing's ten remarks for each
   paper gave 23. The officer opened on each remark counts too. Stage 1 now
   says that the standing query counts each paper as 21 records.
2. In stage 4, the `...Sheet` inside `enclosures` carries no directive, and
   the runner wondered whether it would come back when `detail` is false.
   The briefing now says that the directive on `enclosures` leaves out
   everything inside it.
3. Stage 3 named the three dots with no type an inline fragment before it
   said what they are for. It now says first that they group fields under
   one directive.

The draft still headed a cited remark "not yet identified" for a runner who
did not ask for the endorsement's `reference`. That is how the findings head
every record that comes back without its name or reference, and it is
logged with the other findings above.

## Caveats

- An agent that solves a case shows that the material on screen is
  sufficient. It does not show that a beginner will find it, or how long
  that takes (SDD section 9).
- The runners read long responses through the accessibility tree and the
  Raw response tab, so these runs say little about the Findings tab for 120
  records.
- No run took a hint, so the hint ladders of the two cases are untested.
- The persona of the novice runs remembers _The Corridor Book_. A player who
  does not remember cursors would find stage 1 harder.
